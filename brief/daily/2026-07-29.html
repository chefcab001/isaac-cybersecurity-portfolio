Executive Threat Overview

The current threat picture changed materially during this reporting window:

  • NEW, VERIFIED, ACTIVELY EXPLOITED: CISA added CVE-2026-16812, a maximum-severity operating-system command-injection vulnerability in on-premises Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog. The vendor says exploitation requires network access to the VCO web interface but no credentials. The applicable federal deadline is July 30.
  • NEW KEV, VERIFIED: CISA also added Fortinet FortiOS CVE-2025-68686, which can bypass a previous fix for malicious symbolic-link persistence. This is not a standalone initial-access path: the attacker must first compromise the product at the filesystem level. The applicable federal deadline is August 10.
  • NEW CRITICAL PATCH PRIORITY: JetBrains disclosed unauthenticated remote code execution in every TeamCity On-Premises version through CVE-2026-63077. Fixed releases are 2025.11.7 and 2026.1.3. JetBrains had no evidence of active exploitation when it published the advisory.
  • EMERGING, RESEARCHER-REPORTED EXPLOITATION: Fastjson 1.x CVE-2026-16723 enables remote code execution in versions 1.2.68 through 1.2.83 under specific Spring Boot deployment conditions. Alibaba confirms the vulnerability; current exploitation reporting comes from security researchers and vendors rather than CISA KEV.
  • UPDATED RANSOMWARE INTELLIGENCE: Reporting now associates active exploitation of PTC Windchill and FlexPLM CVE-2026-12569 with Cl0p affiliates. The vulnerability and exploitation were already established; the ransomware association is the material update.
  • UPDATED BREACH SCOPE: Origin Energy now believes information belonging to approximately 900,000 current and former customers was accessed. Possible fields include identity, contact, account, and partial-payment details. This supersedes the previous unresolved population estimate.
  • NEW, PARTIALLY VERIFIED INCIDENT: Bank of Baroda confirmed that compromise of an employee email account enabled unauthorized access to certain data. The bank says its core banking systems were not accessed. Claims of a 700 GB to 1 TB leak and specific data categories remain externally reported, not bank-confirmed.
  • UPDATED HEALTHCARE OPERATIONS: Most AnMed physician offices reopened for July 28, but imaging, heart and vascular diagnostics, radiation oncology, women’s diagnostics, and several other services remained closed or limited while the malware-related investigation continued.
  • NEW OT/ICS BATCH: CISA issued seven additional ICS advisories on July 28 covering Siemens, MikroTik, igloohome, and ABB products. Combined with the seven July 23 advisories, fourteen recent OT/ICS records require product-specific applicability review.
  • NEW AI AND GRC MATERIAL: CIS published a Controls v8.1.2 AI Security Guidance Workbook, the Model Context Protocol project released specification version 2026-07-28, Microsoft announced agentic-defense and AI red-team initiatives, and NVIDIA and founding partners launched the Open Secure AI Alliance. These are governance, architecture, and industry-coordination developments, not proof that agentic security tools are safe or effective by default.

Continuing high-priority threats remain visible: the SonicWall SMA1000 vulnerabilities, Palo Alto Networks GlobalProtect, Check Point management servers, on-premises SharePoint, Zimbra, Chrome, fairlife, Craneware, DentaQuest, the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident, and FedRAMP Notice 0016.

Since Yesterday

NEW: Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day enters KEV

Arista published Security Advisory 0144 on July 27 for CVE-2026-16812, an OS command-injection vulnerability affecting on-premises VeloCloud Orchestrator. The vendor rates it CVSS v3.1 and v4.0 10.0 and states that it is actively exploited.

Affected releases are:

  • VCO 5.2.x before 5.2.3.14
  • VCO 6.1.x before 6.1.3.4
  • VCO 6.4.x before 6.4.2.4
  • VCO 7.0.x before 7.0.0.1

Hosted and Dedicated VCO versions were patched before the advisory. VeloCloud Gateway and Edge are not identified as directly affected products, although compromise of the orchestrator may expose managed devices and associated credentials or key material.

Applicability: On-premises VCO deployments reachable over HTTP(S). VCO credentials are not required for exploitation.

Actions

  • REQUIRED: Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies must apply the applicable CISA KEV and BOD 26-04 actions by July 30.
  • RECOMMENDED: Upgrade to the vendor-fixed release for the deployed train. Until complete, restrict the web interface to trusted administrative networks.
  • RECOMMENDED: Review VCO web, backend, system, database, and filesystem evidence for unexpected requests, outbound activity, command execution, file creation, database export, archive creation, configuration changes, and privileged maintenance actions.
  • CONDITIONAL: If compromise is suspected, preserve evidence before remediation, rotate relevant credentials and keys, validate managed-device state, and rebuild or replace the orchestrator from trusted sources where appropriate.

Risk if ignored: Compromise of the orchestrator host, its data, credentials, certificates, keys, device inventory, and potentially downstream VeloCloud Edge administration.

Evidence: Arista Security Advisory 0144, NVD CVE-2026-16812, and CISA July 27 KEV alert. Vendor advisory and CVE published July 27; last verified July 28, 2026. Source classification: Tier 2 vendor/CNA and Tier 1 identifier and government exploitation records. Lifecycle: NEW, VERIFIED, ACTIVELY EXPLOITED. Confidence: HIGH.

NEW KEV: Fortinet persistence-fix bypass

Fortinet CVE-2025-68686 affects FortiOS 7.6.0–7.6.1, 7.4.0–7.4.6, and listed 7.2, 7.0, and 6.4 branches. A crafted request can bypass a fix intended to prevent persistence through malicious symbolic links.

Fortinet and the CVE record state that an attacker must first have compromised the product at the filesystem level through another vulnerability. This flaw should therefore be treated as a post-compromise persistence and remediation-integrity problem, not proof that an unbreached FortiGate can be compromised through this CVE alone.

Fortinet’s original advisory displayed “Known Exploited: No,” while CISA added the CVE to KEV on July 27 and CISA-ADP changed its exploitation assessment to active. The newer government exploitation determination controls today’s classification; the vendor field appears not to have caught up.

Actions

  • REQUIRED: Applicable FCEB agencies must complete the recorded action by August 10 under BOD 26-04.
  • RECOMMENDED: Upgrade to a fixed FortiOS release identified by the vendor.
  • RECOMMENDED: If the appliance was previously compromised or shows suspicious filesystem activity, perform forensic triage and validate that symbolic-link persistence and unauthorized configuration changes were removed.
  • CONDITIONAL: Rebuild from trusted media and rotate credentials, certificates, API keys, and connected-service secrets when compromise cannot be ruled out.

Evidence: Fortinet FG-IR-25-934, NVD CVE-2025-68686, and CISA July 27 KEV alert. Vendor advisory published February 10; KEV added July 27; last verified July 28, 2026. Source classification: Tier 2 vendor/CNA and Tier 1 government and identifier records. Lifecycle: NEW KEV, VERIFIED; SOURCE-STATUS DISCREPANCY DISCLOSED. Confidence: HIGH for vulnerability and KEV status.

NEW: TeamCity On-Premises unauthenticated RCE

JetBrains disclosed CVE-2026-63077, which allows an unauthenticated attacker with HTTP(S) access to a TeamCity server to bypass authentication checks and execute operating-system commands with the TeamCity server process’s privileges.

Every TeamCity On-Premises version is affected. TeamCity Cloud requires no customer action because JetBrains applied the necessary measures. Fixed on-premises releases are 2025.11.7 and 2026.1.3. A security patch plugin is available for TeamCity 2017.1 and later.

Successful compromise could expose TeamCity data, configuration, stored credentials, build integrity, and downstream CI/CD pipelines. JetBrains said it was not aware of active exploitation when the advisory was published.

Actions

  • RECOMMENDED: Update immediately to 2025.11.7 or 2026.1.3.
  • CONDITIONAL: If immediate upgrade is impossible, apply the vendor security patch plugin and schedule a full upgrade.
  • RECOMMENDED: Restrict TeamCity HTTP(S) access to trusted networks, run the service with minimum privileges, separate servers from build agents, and review administrative, build, artifact, secret, and operating-system activity.
  • RECOMMENDED: Treat unexpected build changes or credential access as a possible software-supply-chain incident.

Evidence: JetBrains CVE-2026-63077 advisory. Published July 27; last verified July 28, 2026. Source classification: Tier 2 vendor and product owner. Lifecycle: NEW, VERIFIED, URGENT PATCH. Exploitation status: NOT ESTABLISHED. Confidence: HIGH.

Fastjson CVE-2026-16723

Alibaba’s CVE record describes remote code execution in Fastjson 1.2.68–1.2.83 under the stock default configuration. The validated exploit chain is deployment-specific: an affected Fastjson version, a Spring Boot executable fat JAR, a network-reachable path that passes attacker-controlled JSON to the parser, and SafeMode disabled.

Alibaba’s CNA score is CVSS v3.1 9.0. NVD has not independently enriched the record. CISA-ADP’s July 23 record said exploitation “none,” while subsequent Imperva, ThreatBook, and media reports describe attacks. The vulnerability is established at HIGH confidence; current exploitation remains researcher and vendor-reported at MODERATE confidence.

Recommended actions: Inventory Java and Spring Boot dependencies; migrate to Fastjson2; if migration cannot occur immediately, enable SafeMode or use the vendor-recommended restricted build; block or monitor malicious JSON patterns as a compensating control; and assess affected applications for command execution and webshell activity.

Sources: Alibaba/Fastjson advisory, NVD CVE-2026-16723, Imperva analysis, and FearsOff technical research. Last verified July 28, 2026.

PTC Windchill and FlexPLM CVE-2026-12569

Sources: PTC active advisory and Help Net Security’s exploitation update. Lifecycle: UPDATED, CONTINUING. Confidence: HIGH for the vulnerability and exploitation; MODERATE for the reported ransomware-affiliate association.

Cloud, Identity and Zero Trust

Today’s edge, email, banking, CI/CD, and AI findings show that identity protection must include:

  • Administrative web interfaces and management planes
  • Session cookies and VPN authentication overrides
  • Employee mailboxes, OAuth grants, and forwarding rules
  • Build-server credentials, signing material, and deployment tokens
  • Device, application, and service-account certificates and keys
  • Agent tool permissions and MCP authorization boundaries

Recommended actions

  • Restrict management interfaces to trusted networks and hardened jump hosts.
  • Require phishing-resistant authentication where supported.
  • Treat service credentials, cookies, tokens, signing keys, and application passcodes as credentials.
  • Detect mailbox forwarding, mass export, unfamiliar OAuth grants, unexpected administrator activity, and nonstandard credential creation.
  • Use short-lived, narrowly scoped machine identities.
  • Separate evaluation, development, CI/CD, corporate, and production trust domains.
  • Require human approval for high-impact agent actions and preserve a replayable audit trail.

These are RECOMMENDED actions unless an applicable law, directive, authorization, contract, or policy makes them mandatory.

Study Today

Severity is not the same as response model

Today’s two new KEV entries demonstrate why vulnerability triage cannot rely on a single score:

  • CVE-2026-16812 is unauthenticated, remotely reachable through the VCO web interface, actively exploited, and capable of total platform impact. The response is immediate exposure reduction, upgrade, and compromise assessment.
  • CVE-2025-68686 has a lower score and requires prior filesystem compromise. The response is patching plus forensic validation that remediation did not leave persistence behind.

The important question is not only, “How severe is the CVE?” It is:

What position in the attack chain does this weakness occupy, what preconditions exist, and what evidence is required to close the risk?

Applying it to VeloCloud Orchestrator, FortiOS, TeamCity, SonicWall, Check Point, SharePoint, and GlobalProtect demonstrates practical ability across vulnerability management, incident response, network security, CI/CD security, evidence governance, and GRC.

Emerging, Conflicting, and Pending Validation

Fastjson exploitation

The vulnerability and affected versions are verified. Active exploitation is reported by security researchers and vendors, but the CVE is not in CISA KEV. Classification: EMERGING; MODERATE confidence for current exploitation.

Bank of Baroda leak volume and contents

The bank confirmed employee-email compromise and unauthorized data access. Reported 700 GB–1 TB volumes, data categories, ransomware name, and affected population remain unconfirmed by the bank. Classification: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

Origin intrusion method and attribution

The approximately 900,000-person estimate and possible data categories are supported. Initial access, actor identity, full timeline, and complete downstream impact remain unresolved. Classification: UPDATED, PENDING FURTHER VALIDATION.

PTC ransomware association

The vulnerability and active exploitation are established. The Cl0p-affiliate association is based on current research reporting rather than PTC or CISA attribution. Classification: RESEARCHER-REPORTED; MODERATE confidence.

AnMed scope

Malware-related network disruption and service effects are confirmed. Ransomware, data compromise, medical-device impact, actor identity, and root cause are not established.

Enrichment limitations

FIRST EPSS record-level values and VulnCheck record-level comparisons were not retrieved for the published CVEs. Unsupported values are omitted. This does not negate independently established vendor, NVD, exploitation, or KEV evidence.

Bottom Line

Today’s immediate priorities are the actively exploited Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day, the newly KEV-listed Fortinet persistence-fix bypass, and the critical TeamCity On-Premises remote-code-execution flaw.

Fastjson and PTC require application and supply-chain teams to act on vulnerable components that may not appear in a traditional endpoint inventory. Origin’s approximately 900,000-person update and Bank of Baroda’s confirmed email-account compromise require disciplined customer protection and evidence-based communication. AnMed’s partial reopening shows that operational recovery can advance while an incident remains active.

The new ICS, CIS AI, MCP, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Apple publications should be converted into owned implementation work, not merely noted as news.

This brief reflects the most complete evidence-supported operational picture reasonably achievable through the documented current-session process. It does not claim that every threat was discovered.
